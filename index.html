How rare outcomes are modeled

Mutation frequency: the formula starts with a 0.42% per-lineage-hour coefficient, multiplied by a stress factor and a colony-front-size factor (0.78–1.2); the current formula peaks at about 1.109% per lineage-hour. With three starter lineages across an eight-hour run, a rough baseline chance of at least one visible variant is about 10–12%, depending on local conditions and the size of each colony. A run can still produce none. This is a game-scale chance that a new visible sublineage establishes, not a literal per-cell or per-division mutation rate; the model does not represent individual cells or generations. For context, a published review puts the E. coli genomic mutation rate around 0.003 mutations per genome per generation, but that biological rate cannot be directly converted into this colony-level game probability.

Stress can modestly increase the chance of a visible variant, but it does not direct which trait mutates. Traits are drawn stochastically, and later environmental fit and local interactions influence how a variant performs. Horizontal transfer is only attempted between close, mixed fictional lineages.

Quorum-like signaling: a second, diffusing signal field accumulates around dense biomass. Some lineages respond when the local signal crosses a threshold, producing a temporary halo/ripple phenotype. This is inspired by quorum sensing, where chemical signals can coordinate gene expression; Tiny Worlds uses fictional signals and thresholds.

Spatial surfing: new variants are seeded in a direction weighted toward open, resource-rich fronts. Their sector-shaped edge can expand disproportionately, but the mutation chance itself is not increased by choosing a helpful trait or guaranteed to survive. Real range expansions can let chance events at the edge occupy large sectors.
